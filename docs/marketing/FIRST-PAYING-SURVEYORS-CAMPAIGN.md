## Decision

Prioritize owner-operated survey practices and small surveying teams with recurring paid work across the UK, Australia, South Africa, UAE and Kenya. Start with guided sample-workflow conversations and subscriptions, using organic distribution. This is a buying hypothesis to validate, not evidence of demand, a sales forecast, or proof of production/field readiness.

The owner wants reliable revenue to fund comfortable hosting. The monthly operating bill is not known yet. Do not invent a required hosting budget or commit to paid advertising.

## First revenue milestone

Current source catalogue and the live public pricing page, inspected in normal Chrome with its currency selector set to KES on 24 October 2026, list Pro at KSh 2,500/month and Team at KSh 4,500/month with 3 included seats. The catalogue is src/lib/subscription/catalog.ts and public pricing is https://metardu.space/pricing . No payment was opened or submitted. Verify checkout, entitlement and support before accepting a customer's payment; this campaign does not establish billing acceptance.

Illustrative planning: reserve 10% of receipts for fees and incidental costs. This is an assumption, not a measured fee or tax rate. It leaves KSh 2,250 per Pro or KSh 4,050 per Team for the model.

Formula: ceiling(monthly costs / (monthly plan price × 0.90)). Subscription counts are scenarios, not expected sales. Revenue is not profit; applicable taxes, refunds, churn and support costs require their own allowance. Team price is per subscription with included seats, not KSh 4,500 per included user.

Make the actual cost worksheet before choosing a sales target: application hosting, database, storage/uploads, backups and restoration, domain/DNS, transactional email, monitoring, payment processing, tax and support. Quote the existing required stack as a whole; do not size hosting for only a static front page. Obtain current provider quotations rather than advertising a speculative hosting price.

## A practical 14-day test

- Days 1–2: choose one verified useful workflow, check the ordinary-user demo/sign-up/checkout path, establish the operating-cost estimate and a way for interested surveyors to request a walkthrough. Use sample data first.
- Days 3–7: publish one strong image and the new short video organically. Have 10–15 individual, permission-based conversations, starting with existing professional contacts. Ask about their last job, present software, repeated office work, and who pays for tools.
- Days 8–10: run 3–5 guided sample walkthroughs. Observe where they struggle. Ask which result they would use and what prevents purchase.
- Days 11–14: invite suitable participants to an existing paid plan only when the demonstrated workflow, payment and support meet their needs. Track actual subscriptions, renewals and support time.

Those activity counts are proposed experiment targets, not forecast conversion rates. Review qualified conversations, completed walkthroughs, paying accounts and retained usage. Likes and views are supporting measures. If interest does not become product use, change the workflow/offer before buying more reach.
